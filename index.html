01

the upgrade cycle

Every Upgrade Leaves
Something Behind.

Planned obsolescence isn't a bug — it's the business model. Phones designed to last 7 years are replaced in 2.5.

1.5 billion smartphones sold yearly — not because 1.5 billion broke.

Glued batteries. Proprietary screws. Software that bricks older hardware on purpose.

02

the local crisis

Local Landfills.
Local Toxins.
Local Consequences.

E-waste is 2% of landfill volume — but 70% of the heavy metals. And the neighborhoods closest to dumps? Disproportionately low-income.

Lead (Pb) Mercury (Hg) Cadmium (Cd) BFRs

→ soil contamination → groundwater → children

03

the movement

Your Device.
Your Choice.
Your Right.

The Right to Repair movement says: if you bought it, you own it — and you deserve to fix it.

40+ U.S. states introduced repair legislation by 2023
EU mandated 10-year spare parts access
2,500+ community repair cafés worldwide
Apple forced to launch Self Service Repair (2022)

05

the global cost

What We Discard,
Others Inherit.

Our "recycled" electronics don't disappear. They travel to where regulations are weakest and labor is cheapest.

Agbogbloshie, Ghana
Children burn circuit boards for copper. No masks. No gloves. Ranked among world's 10 most polluted places.
Guiyu, China
Groundwater lead levels 190× safe limits. Miscarriage rates 6× national average.
New Delhi, India
Acid baths in residential streets to extract gold from motherboards.
